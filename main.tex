\begin{postercard}[yellow50, yellow50, black, black,black]{Introduction}
Enrollment is the most basic measure of a school's size, and Brazil's
\textit{Censo Escolar}, conducted every year by INEP, records it for the
country's basic education schools.

A sudden drop or rise in a school's enrollment may signal a real event,
such as a closure, a merger, or a crisis like the COVID-19 pandemic. But it
may also point to inconsistencies in the official records. Telling these
cases apart matters for anyone who relies on school-level data to study
education in Brazil.

This poster presents ongoing undergraduate research on where, when, and in
which kinds of schools these abrupt changes occur.
\end{postercard}

\begin{postercard}[pink50, pink50, black, black,black]{Objective}
To identify abrupt drops and rises in school enrollment in Brazil between
2011 and 2025, to characterize the schools where they occur, and to examine
how these changes evolve over time, including the pandemic years of 2020
and 2021.
\end{postercard}

\begin{postercard}[green50, green50, black, black,black]{Detecting Abrupt Changes}
For each school, we compute the change in enrollment relative to the
previous year, considering only schools with at least 30 students in the
previous year. Within each year, the quartiles $Q_1$ and $Q_3$ and the
interquartile range (IQR) define the limits $Q_1 - 1.5\,\mathrm{IQR}$ and
$Q_3 + 1.5\,\mathrm{IQR}$. Schools beyond these limits are flagged as
abrupt drops or rises.
\end{postercard}

\begin{postercard}[orange50, orange50, black, black,black]{Data Sources}
We combine two official INEP sources, which are complementary:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Taxas de Rendimento Escolar:} school-level approval,
  retention, and dropout rates. These are \textit{relative} values.
  \item \textbf{Censo Escolar (microdata):} the \textit{absolute} number
  of enrolled students, required to measure changes in school size.
\end{itemize}
Neither source is enough on its own: rates alone cannot show how large a
school is, and counts alone say nothing about student flow.

Both cover 2011 to 2025 and three stages of schooling: early elementary
(AI), late elementary (AF), and high school (EM).
\end{postercard}

\begin{postercard}[green50, green50, black, black,black]{Step 1: Rate Data}
INEP's yearly files were converted to CSV and stacked into one table
(65 columns, 2011--2025). We checked it against the official files, matching
schools by code, and fixed:
\begin{itemize}
  \item columns shifted in 2011--2014, after INEP changed column names and
  order;
  \item school and municipality codes with an inconsistent number of digits;
  \item missing values written as text (\texttt{nan}, \texttt{<NA>}),
  now proper nulls.
\end{itemize}
\end{postercard}

\begin{postercard}[green50, green50, black, black,black]{Step 2: Census Microdata}
Each year has over 200,000 schools, and the file structure changes: one
table (2011--2022), an extra technical-courses file (2023--2024), and six
tables (2025), when enrollment moved to its own table.

We matched equivalent columns across years using INEP's data dictionaries.
Every suggested rename was reviewed by hand, since name similarity produced
false matches. We kept 15 columns: identifiers plus regular and full-time
enrollment for each stage.
\end{postercard}

\begin{postercard}[green50, green50, black, black,black]{Step 3: Merging the Sources}
The two sources were joined by school code and year, with the rate data as
the reference. Only schools present in both were kept (one 2025 school with
no match was dropped).

The registration fields shared by both sources were compared, and gaps and
mismatches were filled from the other source. Six enrollment variables
(regular and full-time, for the three stages) were then added.
\end{postercard}

\begin{postercard}[orange50, orange50, black, black,black]{Final Database}
Rates were converted into student counts: total enrollment (regular +
full-time) $\times$ rate $/\,100$, rounded. Municipality names were
standardized using IBGE codes, and each stage was classified as Regular,
Integral, or Mixed by its share of full-time enrollment.

The final database has \textbf{27 variables}. Checks at every step found
\textbf{no duplicate school-year records}, and \textbf{141,754 schools}
appear in all 15 years.
\end{postercard}

\begin{postercard}[pink50, pink50, black, black,black]{Treating Anomalies}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{36 ``ghost'' schools} were removed.
  \item Nulls and zeros were standardized.
  \item \textbf{26 records} had exactly 1 enrolled student but no approved,
  failed, or dropped-out students. Their enrollment was set to 0, since
  transfers or typing errors are possible but cannot be confirmed.
  \item Schools with 100\% dropout were kept and flagged for specific
  analyses.
\end{itemize}
\end{postercard}
