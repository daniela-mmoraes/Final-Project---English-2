\begin{postercard}[yellow50, yellow50, black, black,black]{Introduction}
Enrollment is the most basic measure of a school's size, and Brazil's
\textit{Censo Escolar}, conducted annually by INEP, records it for the
country's basic education schools.

A sudden drop or rise in a school's enrollment may reflect a real event,
such as a closure, a merger, or a crisis like the COVID-19 pandemic, but it
may also indicate inconsistencies in the official records. Distinguishing
between these cases is essential for any study that relies on school-level
data to examine education in Brazil.

This poster presents ongoing undergraduate research on where, when, and in
which types of schools these abrupt changes occur.
\end{postercard}

\begin{postercard}[pink50, pink50, black, black,black]{Data Sources and Preprocessing}
Two complementary INEP sources covering 2011--2025 were combined: the
\textit{Taxas de Rendimento Escolar}, which provide school-level approval,
retention, and dropout rates, and the \textit{Censo Escolar} microdata,
which provide absolute enrollment counts.

In the rate data, columns shifted in 2011--2014, inconsistent school and
municipality codes, and missing values recorded as text were corrected. In
the microdata, whose structure changes across years, equivalent columns were
matched using INEP's data dictionaries, and all suggested renamings were
reviewed manually. The two sources were then merged by school code and year,
with the rate data as the reference, and missing information was completed
using the other source.

Rates were converted into student counts (enrollment $\times$ rate),
municipality names were standardized using IBGE codes, and each stage was
classified as Regular, Integral, or Mixed. Finally, 36 ``ghost'' schools
were removed, null values and zeros were standardized, and enrollment was
set to zero in 26 records with a single enrolled student and no recorded
outcomes. The resulting database contains 27 variables.

While screening the database for inconsistencies, abrupt increases and
decreases in schools' enrollment were identified. Rather than treating these
variations solely as data-quality issues, we examined them systematically,
as they may reveal recurring patterns and the events behind them. This
became the focus of the investigation presented in the following section.
\end{postercard}
